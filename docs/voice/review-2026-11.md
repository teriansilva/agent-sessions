# Call review, 2026-11-13 (#1996)

The operator's report: calls often give no response, the voice swings between calm and suddenly
excited, noise is transcribed as speech in languages nobody spoke, and answers come late. This
is what a review of the call path found, what was measured, and what changed. Earlier decisions
and measurements are in [`adr-p0.md`](adr-p0.md) and [`p4-hardening.md`](p4-hardening.md); the
rules that came out of this are in [`../invariants/voice-calls.md`](../invariants/voice-calls.md).

The speech servers measured are the operator's own LAN GPU host: faster-whisper
`large-v3-turbo` for speech-to-text and VoxCPM2 for text-to-speech, both behind the
OpenAI-compatible API a call uses. The server-side changes live in that host's own repository.

## Where a turn's time goes

From the Commander's last word to HQ's first:

| Step | Time | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The worker ends the turn | 0.35 s when the turn model takes the sentence for finished, 3 s when it does not | measured through the real worker: the words reached HQ 1.0 s after the last one, or 3.4 s |
| Speech-to-text | 0.3–0.5 s for a 6 s utterance | 0.13 s of it is language detection |
| The commit pause (Natural) | 1.5 s from the stop | HQ drafts its answer during it (#1845) |
| HQ's model, one round | 2.0–4.9 s with thinking, 1.3–2.4 s without | measured with HQ's real call prompt: about 7,000 tokens and 37 tools |
| The call-end judgement | one more model call, after the answer | only for an utterance of 12 words or fewer |
| Text-to-speech, first audio | 0.2–0.6 s streamed | plus the silence the model starts with, see below |

Across 52 recorded call turns in the week before the review, the median from the words reaching
HQ to its stored answer was 4.2 s: 2.5 s for a turn without tools and 7.6 s with. A turn that
searches sessions took 45–117 s.

**The model's reasoning was the largest avoidable part.** The endpoint was set to think before
answering. With a short prompt the same model's first content came after 2.3 s with thinking and
0.2 s without. With HQ's real prompt the gain is smaller, because reading 7,000 tokens costs
about 1.3 s either way: six call questions averaged 2.9 s a round with thinking and 2.2 s
without, and chose the same tools both ways. A short utterance paid for thinking twice, once
for the answer and once for the call-end judgement.

**Changed:** calls have their own thinking level and their own model (Settings → HQ calls;
the operator asked for both to be settings). The default is no extended thinking on HQ's model.
They apply to every request a spoken turn makes (each tool round, the call-end judgement, the
tools-refused fallback, a session search) and to the spoken-confirmation judge. Typed HQ turns
keep the level and model the operator set for HQ.

**The turn model's wait came before HQ heard anything.** Pipecat's turn model ends a turn at
once when the sentence sounds finished, and otherwise waits for more: 3 s by default. "Thanks.
That helps." was taken for unfinished, so its words reached HQ 3.4 s after the last one. HQ's
answer is a draft for the commit pause anyway, and later words join it (#1845), so the long
wait bought nothing.

**Changed:** the worker waits 1 s on a sentence it takes for unfinished, and reports the stop
as the instant the voice detector ended the last segment, so the commit pause is the
Commander's own silence and the wait never comes on top of it.

### Measured end to end

Ten calls through the real worker and the real speech servers, in a throwaway instance with an
empty fleet: five questions, twice each, spoken by a synthetic voice, first on `main` and then
with these changes. The number is from the question's last audible sample to the first audible
sample of HQ's answer on the call's socket (the browser's own playback buffer comes after it).

| | Median | Fastest | Slowest | Second pass only (endpoint warm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before | 6.6 s | 5.2 s | 9.0 s | 5.2–8.1 s, mean 6.5 s |
| After | 5.9 s | 5.0 s | 11.4 s | 5.0–5.6 s, mean 5.3 s |

About a second is gone, and the spread on a warm endpoint is much narrower: the two 8 s turns
before were the sentence the turn model took for unfinished. It is still five seconds. Of
those, about 1 s is the worker and the transcript, 3.2–4.6 s is HQ's model (two requests for a
short utterance: the answer, then the call-end judgement) and 0.6 s is the first audio. The two
slowest calls after the change were the first two of the run, where the model alone took 9 and
10 s; that is the endpoint, not the call.

**What would take the next seconds off, none of it done here:** speaking the first sentence
while the model is still writing the rest; saying the answer before the call-end judgement
returns instead of after; and a shorter prompt for calls (every round reads about 7,000 tokens
and 37 tool descriptions, which costs about a second before the first token). A faster model
can be tried today with **Model on calls**; it has to accept HQ's tools.

## Why the voice changed mid-reply

Every request to the text-to-speech server is voiced on its own: the model picks a pitch and a
pace per request. Two things made that audible.

| Measured (432 renders, six voices) | Pitch outliers | Under 0.8 speaker similarity |
|---|---|---|
| Hi-fi cloning, a sentence of six words or more | 0 of 72 | 0 of 72 |
| Hi-fi cloning, a piece of one to five words | 12 of 72 | 6 of 72 |
| Reference-only cloning, a sentence of six words or more | 2 of 72 | 7 of 72 |
| Reference-only cloning, a piece of one to five words | 15 of 72 | 12 of 72 |

- **The worker cut every reply's first piece to seven words**, to get early audio out of a slow
  server. "Understood, Commander." as a request of its own came out at 499 Hz from a voice that
  speaks at 110 Hz.
- **The speech server that calls had been using for a week clones a voice from its reference
  clip only.** That mode drifts on whole sentences too. The server calls use again since this
  review clones in the model's hi-fi mode, and no full sentence drifted there.

**Changed:** on a server that streams, the worker sends whole sentences, up to 40 words a
request. It learns whether the server streams from the sentences it has already spoken. On a
server that sends a sentence only when it is finished, the short first piece stays.

Hi-fi cloning has one cost: the model starts each render with silence, 0.28 s at the median and
up to 1.0 s. The speech server now cuts that lead from a streamed reply (0.03 s left).

## Why noise became words in other languages

| Measured (121 clips: 70 speech, 51 noise) | Result |
|---|---|
| Noise through Whisper without voice-activity detection | 51 of 51 became text ("Thank you.", "You", "Gracias.") |
| Noise with the server's voice-activity filter | 9 of 51, all speech-like: reversed speech, babble, distant speech |
| `no_speech_prob` | 0.0 on every clip, noise included |
| `avg_logprob` | speech -0.12 to -1.08, noise -0.15 to -1.38 |
| Reversed speech | Portuguese text; confined to English and German, English text |

Two conclusions. First, **no score Whisper reports separates noise from speech**, so a threshold
on one would drop real short requests. What still gets through is sound that resembles speech,
and whether it was meant for HQ depends on the conversation. Second, **the language of a short
clip is close to a guess**, and a call drops an utterance heard in a language it does not listen
in (#1807): a wrong guess on real speech costs the Commander an answer.

**Changed on the speech server:** a request can name the languages it expects and the
conversation's language so far. The transcript is then in one of the expected languages, and a
clip under 1.5 s keeps the conversation's language.

**Not changed yet (#1996, phase 2):** the worker sending those two fields, and one focused
judgement per utterance of whether the words were said to HQ.

## Why calls gave no response

Three causes were found in the code and the recordings. None is fixed by this first change.

- **Any start of voice activity discards HQ's answer.** The worker reports an interruption at
  every user turn start, from room noise too. The call then flushes playback and forgets the
  unsaid rest; nothing says it again (#1996, phase 3).
- **A real request heard in the wrong language is dropped** (above; phase 2).
- **A turn that searches takes one to two minutes.** HQ says a status line after 12 s (#1702).
  The search itself is outside this review.

## The noise gate

The browser's gate (#1667) passes anything at or above -55 dBFS and was never meant to judge
what the sound is; that is the worker's voice-activity detector and, behind it, the server's.
No recording of a real call exists to tune either against, because a call's audio is never
kept. The per-answer timing line this change adds is the first record a call leaves; tuning the
gate or the detector needs its own recordings, with the operator's consent to keep them.

## What a call logs now

One line per spoken answer, at INFO, with durations and the call's id and no words:

```
voice call <id> answer: asked 0.41s after the stop, answered in 1.62s, speaking 0.24s later, 2.27s in all
```
